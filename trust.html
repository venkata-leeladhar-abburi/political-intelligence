Before an interview counts

  1. Inside the booth fenceGPS within 250 m of its booth
  2. Long enough to be realSix minutes or more, timed on the phone
  3. No fake locationNo mock-GPS app on the device
  4. A voice not heard beforeMatched against the whole round
  5. Consent recordedThe voter’s spoken yes, kept with the clip
  6. A real answer patternNo straight-lining, no rushed taps
  7. A possible travel speedNobody outruns the road to the next door
  8. Words heard clearlyThe speech engine is sure of the Telugu
  9. Tone resolvedSarcasm settled, or sent to a person
  10. CountedOnly now does it reach a dashboard

Hard to copy. Risks already planned for.

What keeps competitors out as the business grows, and an honest list of what could go wrong, each with its answer already designed in.

What keeps others out

  1. Our own speech engineTelugu transcription and analysis in one pass at about ₹0.04 a minute. A rival renting speech by the minute cannot match our price.
  2. Data that compoundsEvery round adds consented, labelled Telugu answers by issue, seat and tone. The AI gets sharper with each one; a newcomer starts from zero.
  3. History clients won’t leaveEvery round, seat, note and report lives in the platform and is compared round on round. Switching means losing the trend.
  4. Proof as the productSurvey tools collect forms and agencies sell reports. Nobody else ties every number to a verified voice, an AI analyst and the law.

What could go wrong, and the plan

Telugu speech in noisy villages
Benchmarked on real field clips before the build; unclear clips go to a human reviewer, with a paid engine as fallback.
Field agents faking interviews
Nine automatic checks on every interview, plus random back-check calls.
A wrong call hurts a client
Seat ranges with margins and a method note on every report, never single-number promises.
Breaking election or data law
Spoken consent under the DPDP Act and a silence switch that locks publishing in notified windows.
Demand that peaks and falls
Staggered state elections every year, media trackers that run all year, and surveys beyond elections.
